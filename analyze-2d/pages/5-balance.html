Spectral balancing

Each trace is split into overlapping frequency bands. Balancing in time holds each band at a constant level down the record; flattening brings the bands to a common level, which is whitening.

Output taper, Hz

A full pass takes several seconds, so it runs from the button rather than on every slider move.

How the settings are usually chosen

  1. The output taper sits on the band measured on step 2: f2 and f3 at the 6 dB points, f1 and f4 at the 20 dB points. Corners beyond the measured band raise frequencies that hold mostly noise.
  2. Balancing in time between about 0.5 and 0.8 makes up for the loss of high frequencies with depth.
  3. Flattening across frequency is kept moderate, about 0.3 to 0.5. Full flattening is whitening: every band is brought to the same level whether it carries reflections or noise.
  4. The envelope window covers several periods of the band center, about 5 to 15. A short window follows single reflections and evens out real amplitude contrasts between them.
  5. The stability floor, about 5 to 10%, stops bands with almost no energy from being raised without limit.

The checks under the panels show whether frequencies outside the band are being raised, whether the band now holds steady down the record, and whether lateral continuity drops.
